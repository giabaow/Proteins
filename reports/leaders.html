Attomarker

United Kingdom
high confidence attomarker.com

Attomarker develops point-of-care antibody testing devices, including a Triple Antibody Test for COVID-19 that detects antibodies to three different viral proteins in seven minutes.

Mechanism A multiplex platform that simultaneously tests for multiple clinically relevant biomarkers against the SARS-CoV-2 virus: three virus proteins (Spike 1, Spike 2 & N) and three classes of antibodies (IgM, IgG and IgA), giving a quantitative profile of the patient's immune response.

The leader to study

Andrew Shaw CEO & Founder, Attomarker; Associate Professor of Physical Chemistry, University of Exeter

Leads the University of Exeter research laboratory (Prof Shaw's research laboratory) whose translational research over many years produced the gold-nanoparticle plasmon multiplex platform; personally led the team that ran the first patient trial at St Thomas' Hospital in March 2020 and is credited in company materials as the scientist behind the technology's development into the Triple Antibody Test.

Why worth studying Attomarker shows a clear, repeatable playbook for a university spin-out with a physics-based, label-free multiplex protein/antibody detection platform: fast pivot of an EPSRC-funded research idea into a real-world clinical validation (St Thomas' Hospital) within weeks of a crisis, rapid regulatory clearance (MHRA/CE/UKCA), a manufacturing partnership with an established industrial player (Smiths Detection) to scale production, and now diversification into a broader immune-profiling pipeline (Long COVID endotyping, liver, fertility, allergy) plus non-dilutive/crowd equity funding routes (Exeter alumni appeal, Crowdcube Acceleration Round) and disease-area co-development deals (EpitogenX). This is directly analogous to Proteins.1's path from research-use biomarker detection toward regulated diagnostics.

What made them succeed
  • Rapid real-world clinical validation partnership with a major hospitalAttomarker printed gold arrays with minimal validation and began a collaboration with St Thomas' Hospital on 23rd March 2020, obtaining first patient results the same afternoon.
  • Peer-reviewed publication to support credibility and regulatory submissionResults from the St Thomas' pilot (119 tests, 74 patients) were published in the Royal Society of Chemistry journal Analyst, later supplemented by a second paper with 200 more patients showing up to 96% sensitivity.
  • Regulatory clearance milestone (MHRA authorisation) before scale-upThe Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) approved Attomarker's Triple Antibody Test for NHS use in the UK following the St Thomas' trial.
  • Manufacturing partnership with an established industrial player to scale device productionSmiths Detection collaborated with Attomarker since April 2020, manufacturing 10 pre-production devices at its Hemel Hempstead site with ability to ramp up volume, each device testing up to 50 blood samples per day.
  • Non-dilutive community/alumni funding to bootstrap the first clinical trialThe trial was fully funded by the University of Exeter's COVID-19 Emergency Appeal which raised a record £120,000 from over 1,000 alumni.
  • Disease-area co-development deal to extend the platform beyond the original indicationAttomarker recently signed a strategic co-development agreement with EpitogenX to leverage their next generation antigen technology, targeting Lyme disease and Hashimoto's disease.
  • Equity crowdfunding as a growth-stage financing routeAttomarker launched an Acceleration Round hosted on Crowdcube, the UK's regulated equity crowdfunding platform, to support commercial rollout and regulatory progress.
For Proteins.1
Applications to consider
  • Run a rapid 'crisis-anchored' pilot at a single leading hospital or research centre with a small, well-characterised patient cohort to generate a first real-world dataset quickly, as Attomarker did at St Thomas' within days of first contact.
  • Publish early pilot data in a peer-reviewed analytical chemistry/diagnostics journal (e.g. Analyst-type venue) to build third-party credibility ahead of any regulatory submission.
  • Seek a co-manufacturing partner with existing precision-instrument production capability (akin to Smiths Detection) to move from lab prototype to a robust, scalable point-of-need device rather than building manufacturing in-house.
  • Design the platform around a flexible, swappable biomarker panel (as Attomarker did, adding CRP, PAG, and viral proteins rapidly) so the same core physics engine can be re-targeted across oncology, neurology and immunology research panels without a full redesign.
  • Pursue a low-cost, non-dilutive proof-of-concept funding source (alumni/community appeal, translational grant) to fund the first pilot study before seeking larger venture or crowdfunding rounds.
  • Consider equity crowdfunding (e.g. Crowdcube-style rounds) once initial clinical/regulatory milestones are hit, as a route to fund late seed/Series A commercial rollout while building public visibility.
Market-route moves
  • Start in research-use-only sales to academic and hospital labs performing biomarker discovery, using early pilot partnerships (hospital collaborations) as reference sites, mirroring Attomarker's St Thomas' Hospital entry point.
  • Target a first regulatory clearance in a single high-need, well-defined indication (e.g. a specific oncology or neurology biomarker panel) via a fast-track pathway analogous to Attomarker's MHRA/CE Triple Antibody Test authorisation, rather than attempting broad clinical diagnostics clearance at once.
  • License or co-develop with an established diagnostics/instrumentation manufacturer for device production and distribution once a validated assay exists, following the Smiths Detection manufacturing partnership model.
  • Expand indication-by-indication through targeted co-development deals with antigen/reagent specialists (as with EpitogenX) to reach underserved disease markets (e.g. specific neuro-immunology or oncology biomarkers) without needing to build every assay internally.
  • Once clinical validation and initial regulatory milestones are achieved, use a phased financing approach: philanthropic/alumni-style seed funding for pilot studies, followed by an equity crowdfunding or accelerator round to fund commercial rollout and further regulatory work.
Suggested sequence for Proteins.1

Follow Attomarker's precedent: secure a fast, low-cost pilot at a marquee clinical site to generate real-world validation data, publish it to build scientific credibility, and use that evidence to obtain an initial regulatory clearance in a narrowly defined indication. Then partner with an established instrumentation manufacturer (as Attomarker did with Smiths Detection) to scale device production, while expanding the biomarker panel indication-by-indication through targeted co-development deals (as with EpitogenX) and financing growth through a mix of philanthropic seed funding and equity crowdfunding.

Biognosys

Switzerland
high confidence biognosys.com

Biognosys is a proteomics company that provides mass spectrometry-based discovery and targeted proteomics technologies, software, kits, and contract research services for protein expression, function, and structure analysis.

Mechanism Mass spectrometry-based workflows including Data Independent Acquisition (DIA) via their patented Hyper Reaction Monitoring (HRM) technology for discovery proteomics, targeted acquisition methods (PRM, SureQuant, MRM) with stable isotope-labeled standard peptides for absolute quantification, and Limited Proteolysis-Mass Spectrometry (LiP-MS) for probing protein structural changes via altered proteolytic cleavage kinetics.

The leader to study

Oliver Rinner Founder and CEO (Ph.D.)

Identified in company press materials as 'Dr. Oliver Rinner, Founder and CEO of Biognosys.' No further educational or career background is given in the provided text beyond his title and repeated quotes as CEO and Co-Founder driving the company's strategy toward clinical proteomics.

Why worth studying Biognosys shows how a platform-technology company (MS-based proteomics) can build a defensible moat through patents/exclusive licenses (HRM, iRT, LiP-MS), monetize via a layered CRO + software + kit business model (TrueDiscovery/TrueTarget/TrueSignature/Spectronaut/PQ500), and progress from academic-origin tech to biopharma and clinical-trial deployment through sequential strategic partnerships (Bruker, SISCAPA) and funding rounds (Series G) rather than a single big pharma deal - a relevant precedent for Proteins.1 as it moves from research-use amplification/detection technology toward regulated diagnostics.

What made them succeed
  • Exclusive academic technology license (LiP-MS from ETH Zurich)"LiP-MS was invented by Biognosys' scientific advisor Prof. Paola Picotti at ETH Zurich... Biognosys is the sole, exclusive provider of proteomics solutions based on LiP-MS technology."
  • Strategic instrument-platform partnership with Bruker"Biognosys will participate in the activities of its strategic partner, Bruker... A major launch arising from the strategic partnership is the combination of Biognosys' iRT kit together with the Bruker ProteoScape product."
  • Exclusive collaboration with SISCAPA for end-to-end high-sensitivity workflow"SISCAPA Assay Technologies... and Biognosys... today announced an exclusive strategic collaboration to offer biopharma customers a streamlined end-to-end solution for high-sensitivity protein analysis - from early protein biomarker and drug target discovery, all the way to targeted enrichment and protein quantification in preclinical studies and clinical trials."
  • Series G financing to build GCP-certified clinical infrastructure and expand global commercial reach"The funds will be invested into building a new GCP-certified facility in Switzerland, increasing current operational capacity as well as enhancing the company's sales force, further expanding its commercial presence in the US (both East and West Coast), Europe, and Asia."
  • Development of a standardized reference-peptide kit (PQ500) to enable cross-platform comparability for large cohort studies"Our PQ500™ Reference Peptides kit, an optimized set of 804 SIS peptides targeting 582 proteins, offers... greatly improved comparability across analytical platforms, making it especially helpful in large studies and cohort sizes."
For Proteins.1
Applications to consider
  • Package Proteins.1's core detection platform as a tiered service model analogous to TrueDiscovery (broad research discovery) and TrueSignature (targeted, precise biomarker monitoring), letting research customers 'graduate' from exploratory biomarker discovery to validated, reproducible targeted panels.
  • Develop a reference-standard kit (like PQ500) with defined synthetic/spike-in controls for Proteins.1's readout modality to enable cross-lab, cross-instrument reproducibility - critical for adoption in multi-site academic and pharma studies.
  • Seek an exclusive academic license or scientific-advisor relationship (as Biognosys did with Prof. Picotti/ETH Zurich for LiP-MS) to anchor Proteins.1's enzyme-free amplification method in a defensible, patent-protected niche application (e.g., structural/PTM biomarker profiling) that competitors cannot easily replicate.
  • Build software/analysis tooling (a 'Spectronaut'-equivalent) around the raw signal output to lock in customers through a combined hardware+software+consumables ecosystem rather than selling instruments alone.
Market-route moves
  • Start as a CRO/service-and-kit provider to oncology, neurology and immunology researchers (mirroring Biognosys' TrueDiscovery/TrueTarget/TrueSignature CRO model) before pursuing regulated diagnostics, to generate revenue and real-world validation data early.
  • Form a strategic co-marketing/technology partnership with an established instrument or platform vendor (Biognosys-Bruker precedent) to gain distribution credibility and technical integration rather than building a standalone sales channel from scratch.
  • Pursue an exclusive complementary-technology partnership with a specialized enrichment/detection technology provider (as with SISCAPA) to offer an 'end-to-end' discovery-to-clinical-biomarker workflow, increasing stickiness with biopharma clients doing preclinical and clinical trial work.
  • Use a growth-stage funding round explicitly earmarked for building GCP/GLP-compliant infrastructure (as Biognosys' Series G funded a GCP facility) to signal clinical-market readiness to investors and pharma partners before formal regulatory submissions.
Suggested sequence for Proteins.1

Follow Biognosys' precedent: first establish Proteins.1's platform as an indispensable research tool via CRO-style discovery and targeted-quantification services layered with proprietary kits and analysis software, using research-use adoption (oncology, neurology, immunology) to build a large publication and reproducibility track record. In parallel, secure an exclusive academic or technology partnership to protect a specific technical edge (as Biognosys did with LiP-MS/ETH Zurich) and a strategic partnership with an established instrument/enrichment provider (as with Bruker and SISCAPA) to extend reach and credibility. Use a dedicated funding round to build GCP/GLP-compliant infrastructure before formally entering the regulated clinical diagnostics market, mirroring Biognosys' Series G-funded expansion into clinical trials.

Dynamic Biosensors

Germany
high confidence dynamic-biosensors.com

Dynamic Biosensors develops biosensor instruments and assay technologies to help scientists characterize molecular interactions and accelerate drug discovery. The company was acquired by Bruker in 2024, forming the Bruker Biosensors business.

Mechanism switchSENSE® technology combined with single-cell Interaction Cytometry (scIC), run on heliX / heliX+ biosensor instruments (and heliXcyto for cell-based measurements)

The leader to study

Ulrich Rant Co-founder & CEO of Dynamic Biosensors from its founding in 2012 until the 2024 Bruker acquisition; now Chief Scientific Officer of Bruker Biosensors

Rant led Dynamic Biosensors from inception, turning what colleagues describe as 'seemingly crazy ideas to analyze molecular interactions' into commercial products. The company itself was a spinout from Technical University of Munich (TUM), based on research jointly conducted by TUM and Fujitsu, developing the switchSENSE electro-switchable DNA nanolever biosensor technology. After leading the company through product development, commercial expansion and the Bruker acquisition, Rant moved from CEO to CSO to refocus on science and technology.

Why worth studying Dynamic Biosensors is a strong role-model precedent for Proteins.1 because it is a university deep-tech spinout (TUM/Fujitsu, comparable to a VTT spinout) that built a physics-based, label-agnostic biosensor platform (DNA nanolever electro-switching rather than enzymatic amplification) reaching femtomolar sensitivity across proteins, DNA and RNA - conceptually parallel to Proteins.1's enzyme-free, physics-based single-molecule platform. It scaled purely through Research Use Only sales into academic and pharma R&D (oncology, immunology, neurology, drug discovery), built credibility via peer-reviewed publications with top academic labs, raised staged VC rounds from regional/deep-tech investors, and ultimately exited via strategic acquisition into a major analytical-instruments company (Bruker) to gain global commercial reach - a plausible playbook for Proteins.1 to follow from seed stage to scale.

What made them succeed
  • Staged, regionally-anchored VC financing (Series A 2013, Series B 2015) from deep-tech-focused investorsDynamic Biosensors closed a €5m ($6m) series B round featuring Unternehmertum Venture Capital Partners, Bayern Kapital and BayBG, having previously raised a Series A in 2013 from Unternehmertum, Bayern Kapital, Biom and Extorel.

  • Deep, publication-generating academic partnerships that validated the technology and drove visibilityCollaborations with Harald Kolmar's lab at TU Darmstadt, Alessio Ciulli's Centre for Targeted Protein Degradation, and Fraunhofer IZI-BB produced peer-reviewed papers (e.g., in Antibodies, eLife, RSC Chemical Biology) showcasing switchSENSE applications in bispecific antibodies, PROTACs and RNA biology.
  • Clear Research-Use-Only positioning avoiding early regulatory burden while building a broad application baseProduct labeling explicitly states 'For Research Use Only. Not for use in clinical diagnostic procedures,' allowing the company to sell into pharma/academic drug-discovery markets (oncology, immunotherapy, virology, neurology) without navigating clinical regulatory pathways.
  • Differentiated, hard-to-replicate technical capability (world-first dual-color detection, single-cell kinetics) as a commercial moatThe DRX² analyzer is described as the 'world's first double color analyzer of its kind,' and heliXcyto is noted as enabling on-cell kon/koff measurement 'previously not possible,' per testimonial from Prof. Harald Kolmar.

  • Build a portfolio of peer-reviewed collaborations with academic KOLs in oncology, neurology and immunology to generate the kind of steady app-note/publication pipeline DBS used for credibility and lead generation.
  • Plan for an eventual strategic exit or partnership with a larger life-science instrumentation player (a Bruker-, Illumina-, or Thermo-type acquirer) once the RUO platform has commercial traction, using that acquirer's global distribution and support infrastructure to scale - the same route DBS took in 2024.

Build credibility and market pull through joint publications and app notes with leading academic labs, using genuinely novel technical capabilities (e.g., single-molecule/ultra-sensitive detection) as the differentiator. Once RUO traction and application breadth are proven, pursue a strategic partnership or acquisition by a global life-science instrumentation company to scale commercially, deferring regulated clinical diagnostics until after this RUO-stage growth, exactly as Dynamic Biosensors did en route to its 2024 acquisition by Bruker.

Evosep

Denmark
high confidence evosep.com

Evosep develops the Evosep One, a standardized liquid chromatography (LC) platform that integrates sample preparation with LC-MS analysis for high-throughput proteomics workflows.

Mechanism A standardized, robust LC system using low-pressure pumps and the Evotip Pure sample introduction device, which integrates desalting with LC-MS sample introduction and uses a 'partial elution concept' where peptides are eluted while contaminants and large hydrophobic molecules remain on the disposable Evotip.

The leader to study

Ole Vorm Founder

Started working with protein mass spectrometry in 1989; founded his first company Protana in 1997 (first commercial nano-electrospray sources/emitters, first 2D-gel spot-picking robot). Protana was acquired by MDS Proteomics (2000); a management buyout formed Proxeon (2002), which developed the EASY-nLC and was acquired by Thermo Scientific (2010), where the EASY-Spray ion source was invented. In 2014-2017 the team designed the Bruker Daltonics nanoElute. Ole Vorm and team founded Evosep in 2016 and launched Evosep One at HUPO Dublin about 18 months later.

Why worth studying Evosep shows how a founder with 20+ years of deep domain experience and repeated exits/acquisitions (Protana -> MDS Proteomics -> Proxeon -> Thermo Scientific -> Bruker collaboration) can rapidly commercialize a hardware-plus-consumable platform (Evosep One + Evotip) into a standardized, high-throughput research tool, then progressively expand into clinical-adjacent applications (diabetes monitoring, plasma proteome, single-cell proteomics) via an ecosystem of instrument and automation partners rather than going straight to regulated diagnostics.

For Proteins.1

Suggested sequence for Proteins.1

Use disease-specific showcases (e.g., diabetes monitoring from pinprick blood) to build a clinical narrative while remaining research-use-only, setting up either a strategic acquisition by a larger platform company or growth-equity-backed scale-up (as Evosep did with Nordic Capital) as the path to regulated diagnostics.

NanoTemper Technologies

Germany
high confidence nanotempertech.com

Dianthus is a biophysical instrument platform that measures binding affinity between proteins and other molecules (e.g., fragments, ligands, PROTACs) directly in solution. It is used in drug discovery for screening and characterizing hard-to-study targets.

Mechanism Two complementary in-solution technologies: Spectral Shift, which detects binding by measuring spectral shifts in the emission spectrum of a fluorescently labeled target, and TRIC (Temperature Related Intensity Change), which measures fluorescence changes after a brief temperature increase to confirm hits with an orthogonal signal.

The leader to study

Stefan Duhr and Philipp Baaske Co-founders and Co-CEOs

Both were PhD students in the Department of Physics and Center for NanoScience at Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität (LMU) Munich in the late 2000s. Stefan Duhr came from a biochemistry background; Philipp Baaske from physics/engineering. They collaborated on a project doing rapid laser-heated measurements of dsDNA unfolding using a fluorophore, then repurposed the heat/fluorophore principle (building on Charles Soret's 1879 thermodiffusion theory) into a new method for measuring protein-molecule binding affinity in solution, which became MicroScale Thermophoresis (MST) and the basis of NanoTemper.

Why worth studying NanoTemper is a strong role model because it shows how a physics-based, label-based (not enzyme-based) biophysical measurement technology born from an academic PhD project can be built, without early VC funding, into a global instrument company trusted by pharma/biotech and academia, expanding from a single core technology (MST) into a multi-platform ecosystem (Monolith, Prometheus/nanoDSF, Seismos/SAW, Dianthus/Spectral Shift+TRIC) serving drug discovery, with deep penetration into real industry workflows (cited in ~20% of new biotherapeutics development) before any move toward more regulated or specialized use cases.

What made them succeed
  • Bootstrapped growth via non-dilutive grants instead of early VC money"We did not have a business plan... No funding rounds. No VC money. No exit strategy... We started applying to business grants in Munich and found they were getting awarded, so we kept working." (Stefan Duhr)
  • University spin-off support structure (LMU Spin-Off service) enabled early formation"Big thank you to the Spin-Off service of the Ludwig-Maximilians-Universität München... who strongly supported us in our early days."
  • Deliberate technology platform expansion by acquisition and internal development to cover complementary biophysical readouts"The recently acquired Surface Acoustic Wave (SAW) technology measures binding kinetics" and "In 2015, a novel product line represented by the Prometheus NT.48 instrument was launched offering nanoDSF technology," alongside Dianthus's dual Spectral Shift + TRIC readouts.
  • Deliberate US market entry via a dedicated fully-owned subsidiary to capture pharma/biotech demand"NanoTemper Technologies announces the foundation of a fully owned subsidiary in San Francisco, California... to meet the ever increasing demand in the North American market for our MST instruments." (Stefan Duhr, CEO)
  • Deep embedding in real pharma R&D workflows validated by scientific citation volume"our instruments have been cited in more than 15,000 scientific publications. Our products are involved in the development of around 20% of all new biotherapeutics." (Philipp Baaske)
For Proteins.1
Applications to consider
  • Follow NanoTemper's grant-first, VC-later funding path: pursue Finnish/EU non-dilutive innovation grants (e.g., Business Finland, EIC Accelerator, VTT-linked funding) before raising large VC rounds, to preserve technical focus and equity in the seed stage.
  • Design the platform to output two complementary, orthogonal signals (as NanoTemper does with Spectral Shift + TRIC) so that Proteins.1's amplification/reading method can cross-validate hits and rule out artifacts - this builds trust with skeptical pharma/academic buyers evaluating a new physics-based method.
  • Prioritize research-use instrument placement in academic and pharma discovery labs first (oncology, neurology, immunology single-molecule biomarker work) to accumulate publications and citations, mirroring NanoTemper's 15,000+ citation base, before pursuing regulated clinical diagnostics.
  • Build a software/data ecosystem (a 'Hub' style platform with structured, machine-readable exports and APIs) early, so that Proteins.1's biomarker data integrates into existing pharma/biotech analysis pipelines and automation, increasing stickiness similar to NanoTemper's Hub apps and open API strategy.
  • Leverage the VTT spin-off relationship the same way NanoTemper leveraged LMU's spin-off office - use VTT's tech-transfer and grant-writing support to accelerate incorporation, patent filing, and first business-plan competitions.
Market-route moves
  • Enter via the same customer segment NanoTemper targeted first - academic and biotech/pharma discovery labs doing hard-target biomarker/protein characterization - using research-use-only positioning to avoid early regulatory burden.
  • Once technical credibility and publication citations are established in research markets, expand geographically through direct subsidiaries in key biotech hubs (Boston/San Francisco) as NanoTemper did, rather than distributors, to control customer application support.
  • Add complementary detection modalities/product lines over time (as NanoTemper added SAW and nanoDSF) so a single-molecule DNA/RNA/protein biomarker platform becomes a multi-application ecosystem, increasing account penetration before diagnostics regulatory filings.
  • Only pursue regulated clinical diagnostics after the research-use platform has strong adoption data and publication support, using that evidence base to de-risk regulatory submissions, mirroring NanoTemper's decade-plus research-market-first trajectory before any diagnostic-adjacent moves (e.g., diagnostic-grade plate manufacturing).
